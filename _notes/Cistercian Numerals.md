Cistercian numerals are a medieval notation that packs any number from 1 to 9999 into a single glyph built on one vertical stem, useful when you need to record a number compactly rather than compute with it. The system was invented by the Cistercian order of monks in the 13th century, per Giuseppe Frisella's explanation quoted on the page.

## how
A vertical straight line acts as an axis, dividing the plane into four quadrants, each representing one digit place:
- upper right quadrant: units
- upper left quadrant: tens
- lower right quadrant: hundreds
- lower left quadrant: thousands

Marks drawn in each quadrant combine into one glyph, letting a single symbol encode any number from 1 up to 9999 — for example, a four-digit year — in a minimum amount of space.

## gotchas
- The system optimizes for storage, not computation: there's no long division, and even addition and multiplication aren't straightforward.
