Laurent Baumann's four fundamentals for beginner designers — structure, spacing, rhythm, contrast — turn "make it pretty" into a checklist you can apply to a landing page, deck, or app mockup. Reach for it whenever a design feels unfinished but you can't name why.

## how
**Structure** — group content into buckets (e.g. headline / host / logistics for a webinar page) and order the buckets by what the audience should read first; good structure, not decoration, is what makes a layout look designed.

**Spacing**
- Centering in a box: leave at least half the image's shortest dimension as margin.
- Vertically centering text: center on the lowercase letters' height (the x-height), not the full line box.
- Text in a shape (button, label): keep equal space on all four sides, not just above/below.
- Centering polygons: center the circle that passes through each point, not the polygon's bounding box.
- Nested rounded boxes: outer radius = inner radius + padding, so the band between them stays a consistent width.

**Rhythm**
- Repeat colors, shapes, and text styles across a design for unity; use a repeated "book-end" element to mark the start/end of a section.
- Aim for 8–12 words per line for main body text, 5–6 for a sidebar or caption.
- Avoid justified text unless you'll manually fix the gaps; left-aligned is safer and looks better.

**Contrast**
- Text on a colored background needs a contrast ratio of 4.5 or higher.
- Keep a consistent size ratio between heading levels; weight typically decreases as heading level increases.
- When pairing font weights, skip one weight (e.g. regular + semi-bold, not regular + medium) for enough contrast.
- Match icon stroke thickness to adjacent text weight so they read as one unit.

## gotchas
- Good design is invisible — if you can't tell why a layout looks off, check structure first, since beauty follows from organization, not the reverse.
