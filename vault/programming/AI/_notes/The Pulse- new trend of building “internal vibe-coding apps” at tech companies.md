Tech companies are building their own internal vibe-coding platforms ("internal Lovables") so non-engineers can create and share apps on company data. Ramp built Glass and Stripe built Harbor. Worth knowing when weighing build against buying Lovable, Replit or Retool.

## how
Examples:
- Ramp Glass: a four-person team, a vibe-coding platform for non-engineers. Internal systems are plugged in as "connectors" used as data sources. Apps can be shared and modified by others. Ramp has since launched Glass as an external product.
- Stripe Harbor: an "AI-assisted prototyping tool" built by a single engineer in around six weeks.

Reasons the author gathered for building rather than buying:
- Tiny investment: one engineer for Harbor v1, two engineers and a few weeks at Ramp.
- Internal data can be exposed safely, which would be risky with third-party tools.
- Generated apps follow the internal design library, which third-party tools do poorly out of the box.
- Adoption among non-engineers: at Ramp, building a simple tool in Glass is part of new hire onboarding.
- No waiting on vendors, and vendor selection (compliance review, price negotiation) costs comparable effort.
- Engineers upskill on building and maintaining AI infra.

The author expects companies that already run their own coding agent harness in their cloud to follow.

## gotchas
- The author's prediction is opinion, not measured data.
- Only the Ramp and Stripe examples are given; adoption beyond them is "many other mid-sized-and-above companies" with no named examples.
