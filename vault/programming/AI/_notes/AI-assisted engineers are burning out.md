AI-assisted coding replaces the enjoyable, tactile process of writing code with reviewing generated code, which is more cognitively draining and, because it feels fast, tempts people into doing more of it instead of stopping when a task is done. This is a checklist of habits for keeping AI-assisted work sustainable - use it as a personal or team self-check when the pace stops feeling fine.

## how
**Acknowledge your wins**
- Adjust your thinking: AI may generate the code, but you still did the work - you have the right to be proud of it.
- Keep a win-log and share wins with peers regularly.
- Demo your work to your manager or teammates.
- Track hours: it gives you measurable, visible blocks of the work you did.

**Rethink your AI workflow**
- Plan more, review less: always start in "plan" mode, fix errors in the plan before they become code.
- Don't spend too long trying to make it work: if you can't get a good result after 3-4 steps, start over.
- Don't work on multiple tasks in parallel; it adds mental strain and debt.
- Don't run two AI-heavy tasks back to back; let your mind recover between them.
- Decompose tasks: the mental load of a large task doesn't disappear, it's deferred to review time.

**Keep exercising your craft**
- Protect "craft" hours or tasks where you code without agents.
- Know when not to use agents; keep the tasks you enjoy for yourself.
- Use "Ask" mode to navigate unfamiliar code, not to generate the implementation.
- Don't use AI on passion projects.

**Discipline and work-life balance**
- Plan your work hours and don't work outside them.
- Take regular, proper breaks.
- Plan your intention for the day; once done, don't fill the rest with more hard work.

**Find new areas of interest**
- Work directly with users: interviews, feedback loops, identifying real needs.
- Focus on communication and soft skills.
- Explore analytics, prototyping, and hypothesis testing.
- Learn to fine-tune agents and set guardrails that make "vibe coding" sustainable.
- Get good at performance optimization.
